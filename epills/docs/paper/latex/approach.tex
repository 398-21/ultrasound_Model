\section{Approach}
\label{sec:approach}

A detected pill is cropped, embedded by a hybrid CNN--ViT network, and compared by cosine similarity with the
patient's enrolled medicines or with a product catalogue. Decision thresholds are calibrated on validation medicines
only, and an OCR imprint reader adds a second, independent check.

\subsection{Network}
\label{sec:arch}

Figure~\ref{fig:arch} shows our network and Table~\ref{tab:arch} its layers. A VGG16 branch~\citep{simonyan2015vgg}
with CBAM channel and spatial attention~\citep{woo2018cbam} and a ViT-B/16 branch~\citep{dosovitskiy2021vit} see the
same $224{\times}224$ crop: the CNN contributes local texture, colour and edges, the ViT global layout through
self-attention between patches. A capsule block~\citep{sabour2017capsules} turns each branch into a 256-d capsule and
fuses them by dynamic routing: the coupling coefficients start equal and move towards the branch that agrees with the
fused vector. A small transformer encoder~\citep{vaswani2017attention} splits the fused vector into four 64-d tokens
and refines it; a linear head maps the mean token to the embedding $\mathbf{z}=f_\theta(x)$, with
$\lVert\mathbf{z}\rVert=1$ and 256 dimensions. Auxiliary heads predict the pill's shape and colours. All layers after
the backbones use Glorot-uniform initialisation.

\subsection{Training}
\label{sec:training}

\paragraph{Objective.} The embedding is trained with ArcFace~\citep{deng2019arcface} over the 1,710 training
appearance groups (pill types that share imprint, shape, colour and size form one group), plus auxiliary losses:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}=-\log\frac{e^{s\cos(\theta_y+m)}}{e^{s\cos(\theta_y+m)}+\sum_{j\neq y}e^{s\cos\theta_j}}
  +\lambda_s\mathcal{L}_{\text{shape}}+\lambda_c\mathcal{L}_{\text{colour}},
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
with $s=32$, $m=0.3$ and $\lambda_s=\lambda_c=0.2$, where $\theta_j$ is the angle between $\mathbf{z}$ and the $j$-th
class weight, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{shape}}$ is a cross-entropy and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{colour}}$ a binary cross-entropy
(unknown labels ignored). The margin warms up over the first three epochs ($0, 0.1, 0.2$), then stays at $0.3$.

\paragraph{Optimisation.} Table~\ref{tab:train} lists the settings. Training has two phases: in phase 1 the ViT and VGG
blocks 1--3 are frozen; in phase 2 everything trains. Batches hold $P$ groups $\times$ $K{=}4$ photos
each~\citep{hermans2017triplet}. AdamW~\citep{loshchilov2019adamw} uses three learning-rate groups: pretrained
backbones, new layers, and the randomly initialised ArcFace class table. These rates were the decisive choice. Our
first run trained the new layers at $10^{-3}$ and its embedding collapsed to about 3 effective
dimensions\footnote{$\exp$ of the entropy of the share of variance per principal direction: 256 if all directions are
used equally, 1 for a line.}, enough to encode colour and shape but not imprints. Lower rates ($3\times10^{-5}$, then
$10^{-5}$), a faster rate for the class table and the margin warm-up fixed it (\S\ref{sec:ablations},
Figure~\ref{fig:training}).

\paragraph{Augmentation.} Colour jitter is kept small (saturation 0.05, hue 0.0125) because colour identifies pills.
To mimic detector error the crop window is jittered ($\times0.75$--$1.35$, centre shift $\le 10\%$). For other phones
and lighting we add brightness and contrast ($\pm0.3$), white balance ($\pm8\%$), downscaling to 40--160\,px
($p{=}0.3$), JPEG quality 30--90 ($p{=}0.3$) and Gaussian noise ($\sigma{=}0.03$, $p{=}0.2$), and paste a
neighbouring pill partly into the frame ($p{=}0.2$).

\subsection{Open-set decision rule and calibration}
\label{sec:decision}

A patient enrols each medicine $j$ from $k{=}3$ photos; its prototype is
$\mathbf{p}_j=\sum_i f_\theta(x_{j,i})/\lVert\sum_i f_\theta(x_{j,i})\rVert$. For a query with similarities
$s_j=\mathbf{z}^\top\mathbf{p}_j$, sorted $s_{(1)}\ge s_{(2)}$,
\begin{equation}
  \hat y=\begin{cases}
    \textsc{unknown} & s_{(1)}<\tau,\\
    \textsc{retake} & s_{(1)}\ge\tau \text{ and } s_{(1)}-s_{(2)}<m,\\
    \arg\max_j s_j & \text{otherwise,}
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:decision}
\end{equation}
where \textsc{retake} asks the patient to turn the pill over and photograph it again. We calibrate on validation only,
by simulating 1,000 patients with 10 medicines each. $\tau=0.895$ rejects 95\% of medicines that are not the
patient's. As Figure~\ref{fig:calib}a shows, the margin trades correct names for retakes while wrong names are
already near zero; we take the largest margin with at most 10\% retakes, $m=0.115$. The crop window, $1.55\times$ the
detected box's long side, is the best of a sweep from 1.3 to 2.0, although all windows lie within one point
(Figure~\ref{fig:calib}b).

\subsection{Identification with imprints and calibrated confidence}
\label{sec:identify}

A pill that is not in the gallery is ranked against 4,936 US catalogue products; a product's look score $s$ is the
similarity of its closest reference photo. Florence-2-large~\citep{xiao2024florence2} reads the imprint zero-shot at
four rotations (Florence-2-base on the CPU path). Each recorded imprint token gets an IDF weight in $[0,1]$, so a rare
token (`EC5') counts a lot and a common one (`93', printed on hundreds of Teva pills) little. A product's imprint
score $r$ is the weight of its best token found in the reads, and the top $K{=}100$ products by look are re-ranked by
$s+\alpha r$ with $\alpha=0.05$, both chosen on validation (Figure~\ref{fig:identify}b). In the patient's gallery, only
tokens that distinguish the enrolled imprints from each other confirm or contradict a match. Raw similarities are not
probabilities (pills that are not in the catalogue still score about 0.93), so a logistic model fitted on validation,
in the spirit of Platt scaling~\citep{platt1999probabilistic}, gives the stated confidence
$\sigma(-12.50+12.50\,s_1+72.13\,(s_1-s_2)+3.92\,r_1)$. Here $s_1, s_2$ are the look scores of the top two re-ranked
products and $r_1$ is the imprint score of the first.

\subsection{Implementation and reproducibility}
\label{sec:impl}

We use PyTorch~\citep{paszke2019pytorch} 2.11 and Hugging Face Transformers 5.19, and train on one NVIDIA RTX 4080
SUPER (16\,GB). The model is served as a FastAPI service on a Hugging Face ZeroGPU Space, with 92 automated tests. Each
stage is one script in the supplementary code (\texttt{make\_splits}, \texttt{train\_stage2},
\texttt{calibrate\_stage2}, \texttt{evaluate\_stage2}, \texttt{tune\_identify\_rerank}, \texttt{calibrate\_identify},
\texttt{make\_paper\_figures}), and every number reported here is written to a JSON file by those scripts.

\subsection{Software 3.0 contribution}
\label{sec:sw3-approach}

We used an AI coding agent, Claude Code~\citep{anthropic_claude_code}, throughout, programming in natural language in
the sense of Software~3.0~\citep{karpathy2025software3}. It wrote the model, training, calibration and serving code
and the evaluation scripts from our instructions; we reviewed and ran them. The effective-dimension measure in the
training log located run 1's collapse in the new layers' learning rate, and the fix was proposed and tested in the
same workflow. Every proposal was adopted or rejected on validation measurements, not on the agent's word: two
imprint-reader fine-tunes it set up were rejected (Table~\ref{tab:negative}).
