Final Course Project


The goal of the project is to provide an opportunity to gain in-depth experience developing an AI-based approach to a healthcare problem. Since a large part of the course focuses on deep learning for biomedical and healthcare data, your project must involve training at least one deep learning model on biomedical or healthcare-related data. Simply re-running an existing implementation is not sufficient; you must demonstrate thoughtful problem formulation, model design, training and hyperparameter selection, and empirical analysis. Beyond these requirements, the topic is flexible to encourage exploration of diverse interests.



You may work in groups of 1–2 students, with grades calibrated by group size. The project should take you from a healthcare problem to a neural-network solution and a research-quality evaluation. You should begin by identifying a specific and meaningful healthcare problem, formulate a clear research question or hypothesis, and explain why the problem matters and how your proposed approach addresses it. You are encouraged to use Software 3.0—AI copilots and Generative AI—to help formulate the problem, explore relevant literature and datasets, and develop candidate approaches, but you should critically evaluate these suggestions and explain your design decisions.  You should then develop a Software 2.0 neural-network solution to the problem. Your approach should include appropriate baselines and provide sufficient detail for others to reproduce your results, including data preprocessing, model architecture, training procedure, loss function, optimization, and relevant hyperparameters. You may use Generative AI to assist with coding, debugging, and method design, but you remain responsible for understanding and validating the resulting implementation.



Your experiments should provide evidence for your research question or hypothesis rather than simply report the best model accuracy. Depending on your project, this may include comparisons with baselines, ablation studies, analysis of hyperparameters, robustness or sensitivity experiments, error analysis, model interpretation, or analysis of different data representations. You should use appropriate healthcare metrics and include graphs, tables, and other visualizations that help explain your findings. You should also consider what Software 3.0 contributed to the coding and experimental process, and whether AI assistance improved or changed your development and evaluation workflow.



Project Report


Your project report is due on Oct 25 (Complete and Attach above COVER PAGE to your report). Your paper should be clearly written and nicely formatted, comparable to a published NeurIPS paper, using the NeurIPS template. You are encouraged to use Overleaf (https://www.overleaf.com) for writing your report which may also include visualizations, demos, videos, code, Software 3.0 prompts, or other supplementary materials that you wish to share with the teaching team.

The report should include the following sections:

Title, Authors, Student ID
Abstract. A concise overview of the healthcare problem, approach, contribution, and key results.
(15%) Problem Formulation. Introduce the healthcare problem and explain why it is interesting and important. Describe relevant prior approaches, your research question or hypothesis, your proposed plan, and why your approach is meaningful in this context. Discuss how Software 3.0 (AI copilots) or Gen-AI may have helped with problem formulation.
(15%) Related Work. Describe comprehensively existing related work related to your problem and how your work fits within this landscape. You should connect your discussions with one of the Lecture Recordings (https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLORG7zBQosxw) whenever possible, and highlight relevant Slides/Time in Video Recordings. Have at least 10 relevant citations.
(15%) Data. Describe the data in detail, including its source, characteristics, relevant statistics, preprocessing, and qualitative examples where appropriate.
(25%) Approach. Describe your methods in sufficient detail for others to reproduce your results, including the model architecture, training procedure, and implementation details. Explain your design choices and, where appropriate, how Software 3.0 or Gen-AI contributed to the analysis or method design.
(25%) Experiments. Present experiments that provide evidence for your approach and contribution. Depending on the project, these may include baseline comparisons, ablation studies, hyperparameter analysis, robustness or sensitivity analysis, error analysis, model interpretation, or comparisons of different input representations. Use appropriate metrics and include graphs, tables, and figures to communicate the results clearly. Discuss how Software 3.0 or Gen-AI contributed to coding and experimental evaluation.
(5%) Conclusion. Summarize the key findings, what you learned from the project, limitations, and possible directions for future work.
Submission: Submit your (i) final report as a PDF, (ii) presentation slides in Python code using Lectrace/Edtrace, and (iii) all supplementary material in a single ZIP file. Any code used as a basis for your project must be referenced and cited in the body of the paper. This includes course assignment code, fine-tuning examples, open-source implementations, and GitHub repositories. You may use either a footnote or a full reference/bibliography entry.



Execution-Explanation Presentation Using Lectrace


Use Lectrace to prepare a 10-minute presentation of your mini-project. Begin with 2–3 minutes introducing the healthcare problem, motivation, and approach, followed by 3–5 carefully selected execution states that illustrate how your model produces its results. For each state, briefly explain what is happening, what changes, and why it matters. Do not present the entire execution trace. Instead, select the computational steps that provide the most insight into how your neural program works. Conclude with your key result and its healthcare interpretation. You are also encouraged to experiment creating LecTrace Python scripts using AI coding agents.



We will provide detailed instructions as the deadline approaches and invite students to share the work they have developed during the project.



Project Ideas
You have substantial flexibility in choosing your project. Possible directions include:

Novel problem: Develop a deep learning approach for a new healthcare problem, use a new dataset, or investigate a new task using existing data.
Novel analysis: Reimplement and critically analyze an existing paper, compare algorithmic variants, investigate different input data, interpret an existing model, or predict a different outcome.
Novel methods: Develop a new algorithm or model architecture, combine existing approaches in a new way, or improve the way success is measured.


Regardless of the type of project, grading will be based on the thoughtfulness, thoroughness, and depth of your problem formulation, approach, implementation, and experimental analysis.
